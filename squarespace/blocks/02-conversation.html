Working with Dr. Shabahang

A therapist who engages, challenges, and moves with you.

In individual and couples therapy at AtReef Therapy, I ask direct questions, offer observations, and check whether they fit your experience.

You can disagree, ask why, or say something is not helping. Your perspective belongs in the conversation.

Therapy conversation examples

You might say

“I can handle a lot. I just cannot switch off.”

A question I might ask

“What feels at risk when you let yourself stop?”

We could explore what makes rest difficult, without assuming that ambition itself is the problem.

You might say

“I reach one goal, then immediately feel behind again.”

A question I might ask

“What would make an achievement feel like enough?”

We could explore how much of your self-worth rests on achievement, and what else matters to you.

You might say

“I understand why I do this. I still end up doing it.”

A question I might ask

“What happens just before you fall into the familiar response?”

We could work with the moment insight becomes hard to use, then practice a response that fits your goals.

You might say

“My life looks right on paper. It does not feel like mine.”

A question I might ask

“What do you want that has not had much room in your life?”

We could explore identity, values, and choices without treating uncertainty as something you need to rush past.

You might say

“We explain ourselves over and over. Neither of us feels heard.”

A question I might ask

“What do you most want your partner to understand?”

We could slow the exchange down and make room for both experiences rather than prepare a stronger argument for either side.

You might say

“We make a good team. I miss feeling close.”

A question I might ask

“When do you feel closest, and what gets in the way now?”

We could explore emotional and physical intimacy, including needs that have become difficult to name.

You might say

“I want to trust again. I keep looking for signs I should not.”

A question I might ask

“What would help you tell the difference between reassurance and repair?”

We could clarify what accountability and consistency would need to look like without putting pressure on forgiveness.

You might say

“We still care. We are not sure we want the same future.”

A question I might ask

“What would each of you need to understand before deciding?”

We could examine commitment, values, and the changes each person is willing to make without assuming the answer is to stay.
