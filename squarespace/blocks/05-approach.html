What therapy actually looks like here

Scroll to explore

01

Direction

Set a direction

We decide what deserves attention, what you want to understand, and what you want to change, so the work has a clear direction from the start of therapy.

02

Pattern

See what repeats

We look beneath the immediate problem for patterns that repeat within you, between partners, and across relationships, decisions, work, and daily life.

03

Engagement

Work with what is happening

I do more than listen. I ask direct questions, offer observations, challenge assumptions, and help you examine what is happening as it unfolds in real time.

04

Application

Take it outside the session

We translate insight into real changes in how you respond, communicate, decide, set boundaries, repair conflict, and relate differently in daily life.

05

Review

Review and adjust

We regularly assess whether the work is creating meaningful change. If something is not moving, we name it directly, adjust the approach, and keep going.

How to read the five steps

These five steps are not a checklist or a fixed order. In individual and couples therapy, we may move back and forth between them as new patterns become clearer, circumstances change, or a different part of the work needs attention. The structure gives therapy direction without turning it into a script.

Integrative approach

The work may draw from Gottman Method Couples Therapy, CBT, DBT, relational and Jungian psychology, and Self-Reflective Relationship Therapy when they are useful to your goals.
